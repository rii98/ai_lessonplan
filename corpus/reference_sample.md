# Chapter 2 — The Environment

The environment is everything that surrounds a living organism — the air it
breathes, the water it drinks, the soil it stands on, and every other living thing
around it. Studying the environment means studying how all of these parts fit
together.

## Components of the Environment

The environment is made of two kinds of components. Together they form the world
in which plants, animals, and people live.

### Biotic Components

Biotic components are the **living** parts of the environment — plants, animals,
fungi, and the tiny microorganisms we cannot see. Biotic components are grouped by
how they get their food:

- **Producers** make their own food using sunlight. Green plants like the rice
  plant in a paddy field are producers.
- **Consumers** eat plants or other animals. A goat grazing in a field and a person
  eating rice are both consumers.
- **Decomposers** break down dead plants and animals and return nutrients to the
  soil. Mushrooms growing on a rotting log are decomposers.

### Abiotic Components

Abiotic components are the **non-living** parts of the environment: air, water,
soil, sunlight, and temperature. Although they are not alive, biotic components
cannot survive without them — a rice plant needs soil, water, and sunlight all at
once.

## Local Examples

Around a Nepali village you can see every component of the environment at the same
time: the rice plant (producer) in the paddy field, the goat (consumer) grazing on
the hillside, the mushroom (decomposer) on a rotting log, and the soil, river
water, and sunlight (abiotic) that support them all.

## Summary

A healthy environment depends on the balance between its biotic and abiotic
components. When one part changes — less rain, poorer soil, fewer plants — every
other part is affected.
