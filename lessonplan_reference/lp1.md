Topic: Components of Environment: Abiotic and Biotic Components 

Learning Objectives 

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to: 

Classify components of the environment into abiotic (non-living) and biotic (living) components.  

Identify producers, consumers, and decomposers as groups of biotic components at an introductory level.  

Relate local examples such as rice plant, goat, mushroom, soil, river water, and sunlight to the correct environmental component.  

Previous Knowledge 

Students already know the meaning of living and non-living things and have listed examples from their surroundings.  

Teaching Methods 

Concept mapping  

Group sorting  

Guided discussion  

Board work  

Formative questioning  

Teaching Materials 

Component cards (sunlight, air, water, soil, plants, animals, mushroom, bacteria)  

Board and marker  

Textbook  

Local pictures  

 

5E Lesson Plan 

5E Phase 

Teacher Activities 

Student Activities 

Time 

1. Engage (संलग्न गराउनु) 

- Greet the students. 
- Ask, "What did you see on your way to school today?" 
- Write responses (paddy field, goat, river, tree, bird, sun, mountain, road, house, etc.) on the board without labeling them. 
- Ask students whether all the listed things are the same or different to create curiosity. 

- Share one thing they observed on the way to school. 
- Listen to classmates' responses. 
- Think about whether the objects are living or non-living. 

5 min 

2. Explore (अन्वेषण/घटना अध्ययन गर्नु) 

- Divide students into pairs or small groups. 
- Give each group component cards (sunlight, air, water, soil, rice plant, goat, mushroom, bacteria, stone, butterfly, etc.). 
- Ask them to sort the cards into two groups based on their own understanding without giving the correct answers. 
- Move around the classroom, observe, and ask guiding questions such as: "Why did you place this here?" or "Can it grow?" 

- Work in groups to classify the cards. 
- Discuss and give reasons for their choices. 
- Compare ideas with group members and try to solve the task independently. 

10 min 

3. Explain (व्याख्या गर्नु) 

- Invite groups to present their classifications. 
- Discuss students' answers and correct misconceptions (e.g., clouds move but are not living). 
- Explain that the environment consists of biotic (living) and abiotic (non-living) components. 
- Introduce biotic groups: Producers (rice plant), Consumers (goat), and Decomposers (mushroom, bacteria). 
- Draw a simple concept map on the board. 

- Present group findings. 
- Listen carefully to explanations. 
- Ask questions if confused. 
- Copy the concept map and key definitions into their notebooks. 

15 min 

4. Expand (विस्तार गर्नु) 

- Ask students to identify examples from their home or school environment. 
- Give situations like: "A rice field contains soil, sunlight, water, rice plants, goats, and mushrooms. Classify each."  
- Encourage students to explain why each belongs to abiotic or biotic components and identify producers, consumers, and decomposers. 

- Apply the new concept to local examples. 
- Classify objects correctly. 
- Explain their reasoning using the learned concepts. 

10 min 

5. Evaluate (मूल्याङ्कन गर्नु) 

- Ask oral questions: 
1. What is the environment? 
2. Name one biotic and one abiotic component. 
3. Which is a producer? Consumer? Decomposer? 
4. Is soil biotic or abiotic? Why? 
- Observe students' participation and responses. 

- Answer the teacher's questions. 
- Demonstrate understanding through oral responses and participation. 

5 min 

 

Closure (समापन) – 5 Minutes 

Teacher Activities 

Summarize the lesson by reviewing the meaning of environment, biotic and abiotic components, and the three groups of biotic components.  

Appreciate students' participation.  

Connect today's lesson with the next topic.  

Student Activities 

Listen to the summary.  

Reflect on what they learned.  

Ask final questions if needed.  

 

Evaluation Questions 

What is the environment?  

Name one biotic and one abiotic component found near your school.  

Which biotic component is a producer?  

Give one example of a consumer.  

Name one decomposer found in nature.  

 

Homework 

Draw any four things found around your home in your notebook. 

Write B (Biotic) or A (Abiotic) below each drawing.  

If the object is biotic, write whether it is a Producer, Consumer, or Decomposer.
